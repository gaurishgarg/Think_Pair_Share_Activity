Geometric Proof of the Pythagorean Theorem

Think–Pair–Share Activity • Grade 7 Mathematics

Topic: Proof of Pythagoras Theorem Grade 7 Area-based proof

Learning Outcome

Students will be able to use the concepts of areas to geometrically demonstrate the proof of the Pythagoras theorem.

Common Figure

Both large squares have side (a + b). The four triangles are right triangles with sides a, b, c, where c is the longest side.

1. Think — Individual

Suggested time:3 minutes 03:00

Look at the figure. Both squares are of side (a + b). The triangles are right triangles of sides a, b, c.

For each big square, write:

  1. The area of the shaded region.
  2. The side length(s) of the unshaded square(s).

2. Pair — Discuss and Derive

Suggested time:5–7 minutes 07:00

Compare your Think answers with your partner.

  1. Did you both get the shaded area as 2ab?
  2. What are the side lengths of the unshaded regions?
  3. Find the area of the unshaded region in each big square.
  4. Since the two big squares have equal areas, what relationship can you write?
Pair deliverable: Together, write one equation relating a, b and c, and briefly explain how you obtained it.

Hint

The two big squares have the same side length. The same four triangles are used in both arrangements.

3. Share — Whole Class

Suggested time:8–10 minutes 10:00

Share your equation and explain how you obtained it.

Discuss

Conclusion: The unshaded region in the first arrangement has area c², while the two unshaded squares in the second arrangement have total area a² + b². Since the two big squares and their shaded areas are equal:
c² = a² + b²
This is the Pythagorean theorem.
